# Run the Debate and Decision Engine

Create productive disagreement, not theatrical argument. Preserve independent judgment, expose assumptions, and end with a decision that names evidence, uncertainty, ownership, and revisit conditions.

Read `../../docs/decision-intelligence.md` when it is present and the decision warrants a full protocol.

## Decide whether to debate

- Use direct inspection or a deterministic test when it can settle the question more reliably.
- Use a lightweight comparison for reversible, low-impact decisions.
- Activate a council debate when options are credible, consequences are material, uncertainty is real, and different disciplines see different failure modes.
- Do not ask several agents to vote on a fact that an authoritative source can verify.

## Contract the decision

1. State one decision question and the decision owner.
2. Define the outcome, hard constraints, non-goals, deadline, affected parties, and authority.
3. List known options, including the status quo and a reversible experiment when relevant.
4. Define criteria, weights or priority order, risk tolerance, evidence standard, and stop condition before hearing proposals.
5. Record facts, assumptions, unknowns, dependencies, conflicts of interest, and missing expertise.

## Convene independent positions

- Select only councils with a material perspective on the decision.
- Give every advocate the same decision contract and current evidence.
- Request independent initial proposals before sharing other conclusions.
- Assign a challenger to each leading option and a red team to the leading consensus.
- Keep one facilitator neutral and one accountable decision owner.
- Ask subagents for concise claims, evidence, falsifiers, trade-offs, and confidence; never request hidden chain-of-thought.

## Debate in bounded rounds

1. **Propose:** present the option, mechanism, benefits, costs, prerequisites, and failure modes.
2. **Cross-examine:** challenge premises, omitted stakeholders, second-order effects, security, operations, and reversibility.
3. **Falsify:** identify the cheapest observation, prototype, benchmark, or experiment that could disprove the option.
4. **Update:** revise claims and confidence from new evidence rather than defending status.
5. **Synthesize:** combine compatible strengths without creating an incoherent hybrid.
6. **Red-team:** test the current leader with inversion, pre-mortem, abuse cases, and downside scenarios.

Stop when new rounds repeat arguments, the decision criterion is satisfied, a hard gate eliminates alternatives, or the next useful evidence costs more than the decision warrants.

## Decide

- Apply hard safety, permission, legal, compatibility, and resource constraints before scoring.
- Use the simplest decision method that fits: dominance, weighted criteria, expected value, regret, reversible experiment, real-options staging, or explicit risk acceptance.
- Use probabilities as calibrated ranges when evidence supports them; do not manufacture precision.
- Prefer reversible decisions under deep uncertainty and delay irreversible commitment only when learning has real value.
- Name the chosen option, why it wins, what it sacrifices, and why the strongest alternative lost.
- Assign an owner, next action, validation measure, rollback path, and revisit trigger.
- Record dissent that would matter if a premise changes.

## Guard integrity

- Do not let seniority, model confidence, verbosity, persona, or majority vote substitute for evidence.
- Separate advocacy from verification and decision ownership.
- Disclose incentives, source quality, stale evidence, and material uncertainty.
- Protect private, regulated, financial, legal, and security-sensitive information.
- Obtain approval before external commitments, spending, production changes, or irreversible actions.

## Return

Produce:

1. decision question, owner, deadline, and constraints;
2. councils and positions considered;
3. evidence and assumption ledger;
4. option comparison and decisive tests;
5. selected decision with trade-offs and confidence;
6. dissent, residual risks, and invalidating conditions;
7. execution owner, evidence plan, rollback, and review date.

Do not describe consensus as truth. Describe why the decision is currently the best-supported action.
