# Advise Business Strategy

Connect engineering work to customer value and durable economics. Distinguish business hypotheses from evidence and scenario analysis from forecasts.

## Frame the business outcome

1. Identify the customer, job, pain, alternative, buyer, user, and decision process.
2. State the desired business outcome, baseline, time horizon, constraints, and non-goals.
3. Map the value chain, competitive alternatives, distribution, operational dependencies, and technical enablers.
4. Separate verified facts, assumptions, estimates, and decisions.
5. Request current authoritative financial, tax, legal, or market evidence when it could materially change a high-stakes recommendation.

## Analyze the model

- Define value proposition, segment, channel, relationship, revenue, cost, partner, and key-resource assumptions.
- Model acquisition, activation, retention, expansion, referral, support, and churn as connected behaviors.
- Calculate unit economics with explicit formulas, ranges, source dates, and sensitivity: price, gross margin, contribution, acquisition cost, retention, payback, and lifetime value.
- Treat accounting, tax, investment, and finance conclusions as professional-review surfaces; do not present general analysis as regulated advice.
- Compare status quo, minimal experiment, and materially different strategies.
- Include second-order effects on trust, support, fraud, operations, security, accessibility, and technical debt.

## Design pricing and monetization

- Tie packaging to customer value and measurable entitlements.
- Preserve transparent pricing, cancellation, refunds, consent, and billing recovery.
- Avoid dark patterns, surprise fees, artificial scarcity, or claims unsupported by evidence.
- Define metering integrity, authorization, idempotency, audit, proration, tax, currency, and failure handling before implementation.
- Test willingness-to-pay and packaging with ethical experiments; do not confuse stated interest with paid demand.

## Turn strategy into execution

1. Choose the smallest vertical slice that can test the riskiest business assumption.
2. Define leading outcome metrics and guardrails, not vanity metrics.
3. Set an experiment duration, sample assumptions, decision threshold, and stop condition.
4. Translate strategy into product requirements, system constraints, operational ownership, and a reversible rollout.
5. Estimate ranges and identify the variables with the highest sensitivity.
6. Record decision owner, review date, invalidating evidence, and next option.

## Convene councils

Call Product, Engineering, Design, Security, Finance-aware, Growth, and Customer Success perspectives only when material. Use `$run-debate-decisions` for consequential disagreement. Do not let business urgency override user protection or production gates.

## Return

Produce:

1. business question and current evidence;
2. customer, value, and competitive model;
3. options with economics, risks, and second-order effects;
4. recommended strategy and explicit assumptions;
5. experiment, metrics, guardrails, and decision threshold;
6. product and engineering implications;
7. professional-review needs and market facts marked `inferred`, `not_run`, or
   `blocked`.

Do not promise product-market fit, infinite growth, or financial returns.
