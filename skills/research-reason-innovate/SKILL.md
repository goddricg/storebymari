# Research, Reason, and Innovate

Turn curiosity into falsifiable knowledge and options into testable mechanisms. Prefer primary evidence and explicit uncertainty over impressive synthesis.

## Contract the inquiry

1. State the question, decision it serves, scope, time horizon, and evidence standard.
2. Define terms, units, system boundary, stakeholders, and constraints.
3. Separate known facts, priors, assumptions, unknowns, and values.
4. Identify disciplines that materially affect the answer and where qualified human review is required.
5. For current, niche, scientific, medical, legal, financial, or safety-critical facts, search current authoritative primary sources.

## Apply the Wisdom Council

Use the smallest useful combination:

- **First principles:** decompose claims into constraints and mechanisms.
- **Critical thinking:** test source, logic, alternative explanations, and incentives.
- **Second-order thinking:** trace downstream, delayed, and feedback effects.
- **Systems thinking:** map stocks, flows, dependencies, loops, and bottlenecks.
- **Probabilistic/Bayesian thinking:** update ranges from evidence without false precision.
- **Scientific method:** form hypotheses, predictions, controls, and falsifiers.
- **Game theory:** model incentives, information, strategic responses, and equilibrium risks.
- **OODA loop:** observe, orient, decide, act, and update under changing conditions.
- **Inversion/pre-mortem:** ask how the outcome fails.
- **Occam/Pareto/Hanlon:** use as prompts, not laws that override evidence.

## Research

- Prefer original specifications, research, official documentation, datasets, and direct measurements.
- Record source date, version, method, population, uncertainty, and applicability.
- Triangulate material claims and explain disagreement.
- Distinguish correlation, causal evidence, mechanistic plausibility, and speculation.
- Check statistical power, base rates, selection, measurement, confounding, and external validity.
- Do not transfer a finding from physics, biology, neuroscience, psychology, or another domain by metaphor alone.

## Innovate

1. Reframe the outcome and identify the binding constraint.
2. Generate alternatives through recombination, contradiction removal, TRIZ, design thinking, or adjacent-domain search.
3. Include the simplest change, no-change option, and a reversible learning option.
4. Test desirability, feasibility, viability, safety, and operational fit.
5. Prototype the riskiest assumption at minimum cost.
6. Define success, guardrails, failure interpretation, and follow-up experiment.
7. Use `$run-debate-decisions` when strong disciplines disagree.

## Protect integrity

- Do not invent citations, experiments, measurements, consensus, or scientific certainty.
- Do not provide unsafe laboratory, biological, chemical, physical, or clinical execution as casual experimentation.
- Label model-generated novelty as a hypothesis until tested.
- Preserve attribution, licensing, privacy, research ethics, and conflicts of interest.

## Return

Produce:

1. inquiry and decision context;
2. evidence map and source quality;
3. causal/system model;
4. hypotheses and alternatives;
5. recommended experiment or innovation;
6. uncertainty, falsifiers, safety, and ethical limits;
7. decision update and next learning step.
